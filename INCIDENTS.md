## 2026-08-17 — The live premium panel's "Save" is a no-merge `setDoc`: every barber save silently deletes six fields, and the Active toggle mints a `status:'active', active:false` ghost — proven from the served bytes

**Severity:** 🟠 High · **Owner:** alish/salown-panel-resolve · **Status:** 🟡 Open — the fix is implemented and pushed (`FIN-DATED-ROTA-R2c`), **not deployed**; the defect is serving right now · **Affected area:** Team Members editor on the premium `barber-panel` (`whitecrossbarbers-admin` + `whitecrossbarbers-owner`), barber lifecycle, rota cache

**Tags:** `#data-loss` `#normalization` `#deploy` `#shared-infra`

**Discovery:** **not** by a report, a test or a user — nobody has complained. It fell out of an unrelated release blocker. `SALOWN-PANEL-1` asked a narrow question ("is hosting target `salown-admin` live, and does the untracked `salown-panel` serve it?"), and answering it required fetching and de-minifying the bundles the two Whitecross admin targets actually serve. The blocker's own answer was benign — `salown-panel` serves nothing. The chunks fetched along the way were not. This is the quality signal worth keeping: **the defect had been audited in SOURCE three weeks earlier and correctly recorded, and it still took a read of production bytes to learn it was live.**
**Impact:** on the panel Whitecross staff use today, saving a team member writes a *complete replacement* document. Every field the form does not carry is deleted: `status`, `availabilityFrom`, `leaves`, `leaveFrom`, `leaveUntil`, `role`, `services`, `shiftChanges`. So an ordinary colour or hours edit can silently revoke a departed member's `passive` status (making them assignable and publicly bookable again), erase a leave record that Finance reads for wage accrual, erase the `availabilityFrom` boundary that `STAFF-START-A1` depends on, and erase a member's service list. Separately, the Active toggle writes `{active}` alone, producing `status:'active', active:false` — a shape every passive-authority surface reads as ACTIVE while `OccupancyPanel` drops it. Nothing errors, nothing warns, and no screen disagrees with any other.
**Root Cause:** two writes on the same document with two different and both-wrong ideas of what they own.

1. **The save owns too much.** It is `setDoc(doc(db, "tenants/{tid}/barbers", id), {…})` with **two arguments** — no `{merge:true}`. Resolved from the served vendor bundle, not guessed: `o.BN → gu → "setDoc"` with a `kr.none()` precondition, versus `o.mZ → yu → "updateDoc"` and `o.kd → vu → deleteDoc` used elsewhere in the same chunk. A form that renders eight fields therefore *asserts* the document has eight fields. The document has fourteen.
2. **The toggle owns too little.** `updateDoc(barbers/${id}, {active: t})` writes the *derived* boolean and not the *authoritative* `status`, so the two disagree and the reader picks the winner.

The WHY underneath both is the same and is not about `merge`: **`barbers` had no owner.** It was simultaneously the lifecycle record, the availability record, the service list and the rota cache, edited by four surfaces with no contract saying which fields each may write — so "save the form" and "replace the document" were indistinguishable at the call site, and the safe spelling was a stylistic choice rather than an enforced one. That is exactly the defect `FIN-DATED-ROTA-R2c` closes by giving the three rota fields a single server-side publisher; this incident is the same root cause showing up in the six *other* fields nobody had thought to count.
**Bug Class:** SSOT violation — a document with no declared field ownership, written by a full-object `set()` from a partial form. Sibling of the 2026-08-10 propagation lost-update and of `FIN-EFFECTIVEFROM-BACKDATE`: in all three a writer stated something it did not know.
**Resolution:** **none applied to production, and that is the honest status.** The source fix landed 2026-08-16/17 in `FIN-DATED-ROTA-R2c` (+`EV.1`/`EV.2`): the three rota fields are removed from both browser panels and published only by the canonical engine, `toggleBarberActive` writes `status` with `active`, the save is merge-safe, a Firestore write **simulator** pins the field set, and two negative controls fire. All of it is `PUSHED_NOT_LIVE`. The live release is `whitecrossbarbers-admin` `versions/d6b075dced96fe33` / `whitecrossbarbers-owner` (same release, same second, byte-identical, 38 files / 3,340,328 B), deployed **`2026-07-21T14:57:43Z`** — three weeks before the fix existed. **What this pass changed: nothing.** Read-only: no deploy, no Firestore read or write, no code touched. It moved a status, not a system.
**Prevention:** the source guards exist (simulator + negative controls + the single-publisher rules boundary) and are the reason this cannot be reintroduced *after* R2c ships. What this incident adds is a rule about *evidence*, because the guards were already in place and did not help: **a source audit of a browser surface is not evidence about production — fetch the served bundle.** It is cheap (`asset-manifest.json` → the chunks → resolve the minified Firestore aliases against the vendor chunk) and it is the only thing that distinguishes "we fixed it" from "we fixed it and it is still serving the old one". The `PUSHED_NOT_LIVE` / `LIVE_VERIFIED` vocabulary already said this; this is the first time the gap between them was measured in deleted fields. Second rule, narrower: **a Firestore write built from a form must name its fields, never hand over an object** — `set()` without `merge` is a claim of completeness, and a form is never complete.
**Regression Tests:** the source-side gates are R2c's (`barbersWriteShape` simulator + the two negative controls + `staffRota.emulator.test.js`); they pass and prove the fix, which is precisely why they could not catch this — **no test asserts anything about the deployed bundle, and none is added here.** A served-byte gate would be the real prevention and is deliberately not invented under an incident write-up; it is a release-tooling change and belongs to the release, not to this record.
**Related:** commits — none (docs only; `R2c-EV.3`) · roadmap `SALOWN-PANEL-1` (resolved), `SALOWN-PANEL-2` (new), `FIN-DATED-ROTA-R2c` (the fix), `FIN-GHOST-PASSIVE` (folded into R2c) · live chunks `whitecrossbarbers-admin/static/js/928.9d6cf2f5.chunk.js` (Barbers page), `main.621116d2.js` (alias table) · source `whitecross-site/barber-panel/src/pages/Barbers.js`

**What happened / Diagnosis / Fix:** The session's task was to clear the last `STATUS_UNKNOWN` on the `FIN-DATED-ROTA-R2c` release path. `firebase hosting:sites:list` showed `salown-admin` exists; its live channel carries a real `DEPLOY` release of **7 files / 318,078 B** — far too small for `salown-panel/build` (36 files / 8,555,251 B, whose `logo.png` alone is 1.67 MB). Fetching it settled it in one line: `<title>super-admin</title>` served from a Vite `type="module"` bundle, i.e. `alex/super-admin` (`firebase.json` → site `salown-admin`, public `dist`, built locally at the release minute). `salown-panel`'s CRA `index.html` is base-pathed `/app-bundle/`, and that path 404s on `salown.web.app` and hits the super-admin SPA rewrite on `salown-admin.web.app` — the panel is served nowhere. Blocker cleared, and requirement (2) of it ("retire or cut over before the canonical flip") is vacuous. The same sweep then read the *other* target in that panel's `.firebaserc`, `whitecrossbarbers-admin`, which **is** live and **is** the premium panel. Grepping `workingDays` across all 14 live JS chunks returned four hits; `main.js` returned zero, because the page is code-split — a reminder that a single-chunk grep would have produced a confident false negative. The Barbers chunk was identified by its Team Members marker strings and by six `workingDays:` key literals against fifteen dotted reads. The minifier had renamed the Firestore SDK helpers, so `updateDoc`/`setDoc` do not appear as text and a context grep for them found nothing; the aliases were resolved by following `BN:()=>gu` in the vendor chunk to `gu`'s body, which contains the literal `"setDoc"`. Only then was the two-argument call site readable as what it is. Both defects were already known from source and already fixed in source; what was new was that they are serving, on **two** hosting targets rather than one — which also turned §9's "both whitecross targets" from a phrase into a verified count. Two adjacent checks were cleared rather than left hanging: the `deleteDoc` path is gated on the component's `isSuperAdmin` prop (matches standing delete policy), and both on-disk `serviceAccountKey.json` admin-SDK keys are untracked/gitignored, absent from every `build/`, and not served — `GET /serviceAccountKey.json` returns 200 with the 657-byte SPA rewrite, not the key.

**Lessons Learned:**
- **A source audit is evidence about the repository, not about production.** Three weeks separated the correct source finding from knowing it was live. The served bundle is the only witness, and fetching it costs minutes.
- **Code-splitting makes a bundle grep a trap.** `main.js` had zero hits and the app had four chunks' worth. Enumerate from `asset-manifest.json`; never conclude from one file.
- **Minification hides call *shape*, not string literals.** Field names survive because they are data; SDK function names do not. Resolve the alias table (`X:()=>y` → `y`'s body) before claiming which write primitive is running — "no `setDoc` in the bundle" is a false negative by construction.
- **`set()` without `merge` is an assertion of completeness, made by a form that cannot know.** The field list to fear is the one the form does not render.
- **Nobody reported this.** A destructive save that deletes lifecycle fields produced no error, no warning and no inconsistency between screens — the same silence as the 2026-08-13 split. Defects that erase rather than corrupt do not generate complaints, so they must be found by reading, not by waiting.
- **Answer the blocker that was asked, then look at what the answer walked past.** The narrow question ("is this panel live?") was benign; the fetch it required was not.

## 2026-08-13 — The platform's first live split recorded £15.00 in the drawer when £20.00 was in it, and gave £34.80 to no tender at all

**Severity:** 🟠 High · **Owner:** alish/jack-split-repair · **Status:** ✅ Resolved — one document repaired, **no deploy** · **Affected area:** admin checkout (split tender), Booking Detail / Receipt Panel, Finance, Reports, Sales

**Tags:** `#normalization` `#data-loss`

**Discovery:** owner reconciliation of the day's till against the panel, hours after the sale. The screens were internally consistent and the customer had been charged correctly, so nothing failed and nothing alerted — a human counted the cash drawer and it did not match.
**Impact:** one checked-out booking (Jack Cunningham, `WCB-1786626543598-bbe7`, £49.80 collected) reported **£15.00 cash** where £20.00 had been taken, and attributed the remaining **£34.80 to no named tender**, so it landed in `other` and Reports printed `⚠ 1 SPLIT ROW CLAMPED`. The customer was charged the right amount, the receipt was right, the period total was right; only the cash/card attribution *within* it was wrong. It could not be reconciled against the drawer, which is the whole purpose of the figure.
**Root Cause:** the sale predates the canonical `paymentAllocation` contract by hours, so its tenders lived in the two legacy scalars only. Both carry a defect the field names actively invite:

1. `splitAmount`'s stored meaning is *"the cash leg of the **tip-inclusive** collected total"*. The operator typed `15` — the only coherent reading of a box labelled "Cash £" that sits one screen after "TIP PAID BY: **Cash**" — meaning £15 toward the **service**. The writer measured it against the tip-inclusive £49.80 and silently handed the £5.00 cash tip to the other leg.
2. `splitSecond` was not required. With no second method named, the £34.80 remainder belonged to no tender, and the one UI element that would have shown the operator the mistake ("CARD: £34.80") never rendered.

The WHY is that two overloaded scalars were being asked to answer a question they cannot express: *which tenders did this payment arrive in*. A field whose name means one thing and whose stored semantics mean another will be filled in correctly by the operator and read incorrectly by every consumer, and no screen will disagree with any other — which is why nothing caught it.
**Bug Class:** legacy compatibility / SSOT violation — a stored scalar whose meaning is not the meaning its label implies, with no field to hold the fact it was missing.
**Resolution:** two separate pieces of work, and the distinction matters. **The source defect was already closed** by `SPLIT-PAYMENT-PARITY-B` (`computePaymentAllocation` resolves the whole allocation at checkout, `splitSecond` is required, the split base is the service and never the tip) — live `422bcb40aab7df89`. This entry records the **data repair** of the one row written before it: a guarded, single-document Firestore transaction setting exactly four paths — `splitAmount "15"→20`, `splitSecond ""→"CARD"`, `paymentAllocationVersion→1`, `paymentAllocation→` the canonical body (service £15.00 cash + £29.80 card, tip £5.00 cash, collected £20.00 / £29.80 / £49.80). Those two scalar values are not a reinterpretation: they are byte-for-byte what `firestoreActions.checkoutBooking` writes **today** for this exact checkout (`legacySplitAmount = collected.cash_p / 100`), so the record now says what a checkout taken one commit later would have said, and the canonical and legacy readers agree instead of merely coexisting. **Nothing else moved:** all 23 `receipt*` fields, `paidAmount`, `tip`, `tipPaymentMethod`, `checkedOutAt`, `status`, every loyalty field and the client's `totalSpent`/`lastVisit` are byte-identical, verified by whole-document hash. No checkout replay, no email, no points awarded or reversed, **no deploy**.
**Prevention:** the source guard is `computePaymentAllocation` + `readCanonicalAllocation`, already live — a split with no `secondMethod` now fails `A7_SPLIT_SECOND_METHOD_REQUIRED` at write time and is refused at read time, so this shape cannot be created again. What this incident adds is the *repair* discipline: the tool (`scripts/repairJackSplitAllocation.cjs`) hard-codes its single target and has no `--tenant`/`--booking`/`--field`, re-verifies the document's sha256 **inside** the transaction, writes the audit record in that same transaction under a derived doc id, and is idempotent. Its own test asserts against the tool's source text that it can never `set()`, never batch, and can never be re-aimed — a one-off repair that could become a backfill is a different and much worse incident.
**Regression Tests:** `scripts/repairJackSplitAllocation.test.ts` — 20 tests. The mirror-parity half asserts the written body is exactly `computePaymentAllocation`'s output for the owner's figures, that `readCanonicalAllocation` accepts it, and that the **legacy** reader independently derives the same legs from the two repaired scalars; the containment half asserts the tool's write vocabulary against its own source. The pre-repair defect is pinned as a characterisation test (`collected.cash_p 1500`, `other_p 3480`, `service.cash_p 1000`).
**Related:** commits `<see repo log>` · roadmap `SPLIT-B-JACK` (source fix `SPLIT-PAYMENT-PARITY-B`, context `SPLIT-B2`) · booking `tenants/whitecross/bookings/3ori9n79QSj09Xyu96fQ` · audit `tenants/whitecross/auditLogs/repair-split-b-jack-3ori9n79QSj09Xyu96fQ` · files `scripts/repairJackSplitAllocation.cjs`, `scripts/repairJackSplitAllocation.test.ts`

**What happened / Diagnosis / Fix:** The pre-state hashed to `b76e344c…6009e495` at `updateTime 2026-08-13T13:12:09.460Z`; the post-state to `7696c275…f30415fd`. Twenty pre-state predicates were required to hold before anything was written, including that `paymentAllocation` was absent and `receiptFailures` empty, and the same hash was re-checked *inside* the transaction so a concurrent edit would abort rather than be overwritten. Every `onUpdate` trigger that can observe the document was read first and shown to return at its first guard: `salownSendLoyaltyEmail` requires the `sendLoyaltyEmail` false→true transition (stored `false`, untouched); `salownNotifyBookingUpdated` requires a status change or a moved `rescheduledAt` (neither exists); `salownNotifyBookingConfirmedPush` and `salownBookingConfirmedEmailTrigger` both require `→CONFIRMED` (the booking is `CHECKED_OUT`); the three surviving `us-central1` whitecross triggers open with an unconditional `return`. Cloud Logging confirms it: all four europe-west2 triggers cold-started on the write and emitted **no application output at all** — no send, no skip, no warning. The client document's own `updateTime` is `13:09:34.513Z`, seven hours before the repair, which proves `totalSpent`/`lastVisit` were never touched more cleanly than any before/after comparison could. Post-write, the live document was re-read and run through the **real** reader modules: canonical and legacy now produce identical legs, `malformed` is false (the `SPLIT ROW CLAMPED` warning is gone), the loyalty redemption stays a transaction-level £3.20 in every filter view, and the Functions-side email renderer — asked what it *would* print, never invoked — produces the same four rows the panel shows. A second dry run proposes zero updates.

**Lessons Learned:**
- A field name that contradicts its stored semantics is not a documentation problem. `splitAmount` meaning "cash leg of the tip-**inclusive** total" while the UI labels it "Cash £" guarantees the operator is right and the reader is wrong, forever, silently.
- Repairing legacy data is not the same as reinterpreting it. The repair only became safe once the new value could be derived from the *current writer* — "what would checkout store for this exact sale today" — rather than argued for from the old field.
- The absence of a fact needs a field. `splitSecond: ""` was not an error state anyone could see; the money simply went to `other`, and only a diagnostic counter (`SPLIT ROW CLAMPED`) ever said so.
- Prove a side effect cannot happen by reading the guard, then prove it did not happen by reading the log. The trigger analysis predicted four silent cold starts; the log showed exactly four silent cold starts. Either alone would have been an assumption.
- A neighbouring document's `updateTime` is the cheapest possible proof that it was not written.

## 2026-08-13 — Cash + Card reported £12.40 of a £9.20 loyalty redemption: a transaction-level figure presented inside a tender view

**Severity:** 🟡 Medium · **Owner:** alish/finance-split-loyalty-filter · **Status:** ✅ Resolved — live `422bcb40aab7df89` · **Affected area:** Finance daily ledger + Reports → Breakdown, payment (cash/card) filter

**Tags:** `#normalization`

**Discovery:** authenticated owner-facing smoke of the `SPLIT-B`/`B1` release, on the same day it shipped. Not a customer report, not a test — a human read the three filter states in a row and noticed that two of them could not both be true.
**Impact:** with the payment filter on **Cash** the day showed `⭐ Loyalty −£3.20`; on **Card**, `−£9.20`; on **All**, the correct `−£9.20`. Anyone reconciling the day by reading the two method views and adding them saw **£12.40** of redemption against £9.20 actually granted. Display only — nothing stored was wrong, the All view was right, and no P&L, wage or payout figure was affected.
**Root Cause:** `SPLIT-B1` established that a split sale legitimately belongs to **both** filtered views (`rowMatchesTenderFilter` matches on leg presence) and that what such a row *contributes* must be restricted to the selected legs (`selectTender`). That reasoning was applied to the tender figures and to nothing else. Both pages then kept summing a **transaction-level** quantity over rows the filter had already narrowed —

```
Finance.tsx   loyaltyDiscount += parsePrice(b.loyaltyRedeemedValue)
Reports.tsx   loyalty         += pp(b.loyaltyRedeemedValue)
```

— so the one live split sale's whole £3.20 redemption was added in full on each side. The WHY is that "filter the rows" and "restrict what a row contributes" are two different corrections, and only the second one had a name. Once a row can appear in two views, **every** figure summed over those rows needs an explicit scope, not just the ones that look like money-in-a-drawer.
**Bug Class:** SSOT violation — one quantity presented under two incompatible scopes (transaction vs tender) on one screen, with no label distinguishing them.
**Resolution:** new pure `src/utils/financeSummary.ts`. `summariseTransactions` returns the transaction-level facts for the rows in view **plus** `additiveAcrossFilters` (false for every tender view, deliberately, even on a day with no split), `splitRowCount` and `splitLoyaltyRedeemed_p`. Presentation change only, tender views only: the Finance chip gains ` (whole transactions)` and a scope line naming the overlapping sales; the Reports totals bar extends the `(whole transactions)` suffix Net already carried to Gross / Discount / Loyalty / Tips, and the booking count reads `N bookings IN THIS VIEW … 1 SPLIT SALE ALSO IN THE OTHER VIEW`. Deployed `hosting:salown` only, source `562148d`, previous `84eb7dda5e1b2140`. **No cash/card share of a redemption was invented**, and no production document was written.
**Prevention:** the arithmetic left both pages. A page can no longer sum a transaction figure over filtered rows without receiving `additiveAcrossFilters: false` alongside it, and `summariseSelectedTenders` still refuses to return a revenue figure at all — the two halves cannot be confused because neither will hand you the other's number. The suite additionally asserts on the **component source** that both screens call the helper and that neither contains a `loyaltyCash`/`cardLoyalty`-shaped identifier, so a future inline re-derivation fails a test instead of reaching the owner.
**Regression Tests:** `src/utils/financeSummary.test.ts` — 30 tests, including `THE DEFECT — the arithmetic the pages used before this change::reproduces the exact live figures` (asserts 320 / 920 / **1240** pence against the pre-change line) and `both screens are actually wired to it`.
**Related:** commits `562148d` · roadmap `SPLIT-B2` (`SPLIT-B`/`SPLIT-B-JACK` context) · ledger `R-2026-08-13-Z` · files `src/utils/financeSummary.ts`, `src/pages/Finance.tsx`, `src/pages/Reports.tsx`

**What happened / Diagnosis / Fix:** The fix that produced this bug was itself correct and is still in place. `SPLIT-PAYMENT-PARITY-B1` had closed a genuinely worse defect — a cash-only view reporting £29.80 of card money — by separating three quantities by name: transaction revenue, selected-method tender, and the full breakdown. The header of `tenderSelection.ts` even states that filtered revenue must never be added across filters. What it did not do was reach the other transaction-level figures on the same screens, and loyalty was one of them. The diagnosis needed no instrumentation: the three filter states were read, `320 + 920 ≠ 920` was arithmetic, and the sanitised fixture day (one split sale + two card-only redemptions) reproduced 320 / 920 / 1240 exactly on the first run against the pages' own pre-change line.

The interesting decision was what to *do* with the figure. Splitting the redemption pro-rata across the legs was rejected outright: a redemption reduces the sale **before** any tender exists — that is precisely why the customer then pays less — so a "cash share" and a "card share" would be two numbers with no referent. Invented money is much harder to catch later than absent money. Hiding the badge under a filter was also rejected: the owner uses it to see which sales in a view carried a redemption. So the figure stays and the scope is stated. `additiveAcrossFilters` is hardcoded false for any tender view rather than computed from whether today happens to contain a split sale — a figure that is safe to add on Tuesday and unsafe on Wednesday teaches the wrong habit, and the day it changes is the day nobody is looking.

Jack's booking was **not** repaired as part of this and remains the unrepaired legacy row (`SPLIT-B-JACK`). That it is still legacy is visible in production: Reports renders `⚠ 1 SPLIT ROW CLAMPED`, which only appears when the reader returns `malformed: true`.

**Lessons Learned:**
- A filter that can place one row in two views changes the meaning of **every** figure summed over those rows — not only the ones that look like tender. Enumerate them when the filter changes, not when a reader notices.
- When a fix names three quantities to keep them apart, check what *else* on the screen is one of those quantities. `B1` wrote the rule down correctly and applied it to two figures out of six.
- A number that is correct in isolation can still be wrong on a screen. Both filtered views were internally honest; only reading them together showed the defect — which is exactly what the owner does.
- The owner-facing smoke found this hours after a green release. Gates prove a change does what it says; only someone reading the screen proves the screen adds up.

## 2026-08-13 — A dated `shiftChanges` override outranked lifecycle: a departed barber stayed visible and bookable on the public site

**Severity:** 🟠 High · **Owner:** alish/passive-r3-split-release (salOWN half) → alish/rel4-wc-passive-hotfix (premium half) · **Status:** ✅ Resolved — both halves live · **Affected area:** barber availability — public booking (whitecrossbarbers.com) + every salOWN display surface

**Discovery:** found during `PASSIVE-R3` source review, not by a customer report and not by a test. Nothing was monitoring it. Worth naming as a quality signal: the defect had been shipped for months and the only reason it surfaced is that someone read the precedence order out loud.
**Impact:** a barber who had LEFT the salon could still be picked on the public booking page, with real clickable time slots behind their button — a customer could book an appointment with someone who no longer works there.
**Root Cause:** **nothing clears `barber.shiftChanges` when the salon deactivates someone.** A departed barber keeps every dated override ever written on their document, including future-dated ones. Both availability paths read those overrides **before** the lifecycle status, so the stale data won: `_shouldShowBarber` ended with a scan for any open entry dated today-or-later and **returned true on a hit** — a branch reached only for `active === false` docs, i.e. exactly the departed ones; `getBarberScheduleForDay` returned the override's hours as the day's schedule before it ever looked at `active`/`status`. The two functions agreed — on the wrong answer — because the same rule was written out twice, in two different expressions.
**Bug Class:** Precedence inversion over stale data — a *dated* fact (which hours) was allowed to answer a *lifecycle* question (whether this person still works here), with no cleanup path making the stale fact go away.
**Resolution:** ONE shared gate, `isBarberPassive()`, called first in both paths, deliberately shared so the button list and the slots behind the button cannot drift into disagreeing.
